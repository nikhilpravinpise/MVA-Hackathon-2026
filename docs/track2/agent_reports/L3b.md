**Ranking rule (verbatim in portfolio.json):** rank by (1) shortest
distance from the evidence to a BubR1-insufficient human system,
(2) pediatric feasibility (labeled use + recorded phenotype-specific
risks), (3) cheapness/decisiveness of falsification. Engine placement is
reported, not used, because the engine scores evidence density in
drug-target databases - dominated by oncology cytotoxics and blind to
mechanisms outside the disease module.

## Why the order did not change after integrating engine + PRISM + ESM

- **Engine** corroborates the mTOR axis placement (sirolimus #21,
  everolimus #36 are its top non-cytotoxic hits) but cannot rank the
  actual leads: D+Q is a pair whose second half is unapproved, and
  niacin/nicotinamide and HCQ/CQ were never even collected - their
  drug-target profiles do not intersect the STRING module. This is the
  documented structural blind spot, not a contradiction.
- **PRISM** (588 launched compounds re-analysed, reproduced Cohen-Sharir
  at Spearman 0.72): **no launched drug is FDR-significant for aneuploidy
  selectivity** (min q = 0.069). Dasatinib shows only directional,
  non-significant signal (low-BUB1B beta +0.037 q 0.24; aneuploidy beta
  -0.0018 q 0.63) - recorded as hypothesis-level corroboration, not a
  rank driver. mTOR drugs show no aneuploidy selectivity (consistent with
  their senomorphic role). **Metformin, chloroquine, hydroxychloroquine
  are absent from the screen - no PRISM claim is made for them.**
- **ESM-2** concerns the allele, not any drug: 650M scores N1002K at the
  88th deleteriousness percentile vs gnomAD BUB1B missense (LLR -5.81),
  150M scores it mid-distribution (48th pct, LLR -0.37). The disagreement
  is stated in the lead-3 `why` as supporting-not-deciding evidence for
  the Axis-A premise (a constrained, function-impaired residual allele).
- Net: the new computational evidence is directionally consistent with
  the curated order and nowhere strong enough to reorder it; the report
  states each direction honestly.
